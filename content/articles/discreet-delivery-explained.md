"Discreet delivery" is one of the most common phrases on a product listing and
one of the least explained. It can mean anything from a plain outer box to a
legal requirement, and the difference matters if discretion is the reason you are
buying online rather than in person.

Here is what the term actually covers, in the order things happen.

## What "discreet" usually means

At minimum, a genuinely discreet shipment has four characteristics:

1. **Plain outer packaging.** Brown box or grey mailer, no product imagery, no
   brand logo, no descriptive text.
2. **A neutral sender name.** The return address shows either the parent company
   name, a fulfilment partner, or a generic trading name — not the brand you
   recognise from the website.
3. **No exterior description of contents.** The customs declaration, where one is
   required, carries a generic category such as "personal care item" or
   "wellness device" rather than a brand or product description.
4. **A neutral billing descriptor.** This is the one people forget, and the one
   that causes the most trouble.

## The billing descriptor is where discretion breaks down

The line that appears on your bank or card statement is set by the payment
processor, not by the shop, and it is often an unfamiliar company name rather than
the brand you bought from. This is normal and it is not a sign of fraud — but it
does mean that a payment can be perfectly discreet in its packaging and not
discreet at all on a statement someone else might read.

**What you can do about it:**

- Many retailers let you set the descriptor or at least tell you what it will be
  before you pay. If discretion on your statement matters, ask before ordering.
- Prepaid cards and virtual card numbers break the link between the descriptor
  and your main account. This is a legitimate use of them, though you should
  check the card's consumer protections before relying on it.
- Payment methods that settle through a wallet rather than a card statement show
  the wallet provider's name instead.

## Shipping labels and tracking

If tracking is included — and for international orders it usually is — the
carrier's own record will typically be more descriptive than the label on the
box. Tracking pages may reference the sender's registered business name.

This is worth knowing if you share a delivery address or a tracking link with
anyone. The physical package and the digital trail are two separate problems, and
solving one does not solve the other.

## Customs and international orders

For cross-border shipments, a customs declaration is a legal document and cannot
be falsified. What is declared must be accurate. In practice, competent retailers
use a category that is truthful and non-descriptive — that is the correct
approach, and it is what you should expect.

Two things do go wrong internationally:

- **The parcel may be opened.** Customs authorities in most jurisdictions may
  inspect any shipment. That is outside the retailer's control.
- **Duty may be charged.** If it is, the carrier invoices you, sometimes by post
  weeks later. That correspondence will be more descriptive than the original
  packaging.

## The honest limits

Discretion at the delivery stage covers the box, the label and the statement
descriptor. It does not cover:

- a household member opening your post;
- a shared email inbox receiving the order confirmation;
- browser history or a logged-in account on a shared device;
- a carrier's doorbell camera or a neighbour signing for the parcel.

No retailer can promise more than the first list, and any that claims otherwise
is overpromising.

## Questions worth asking before you order

- What will appear on my card statement?
- What name will be on the return address?
- What will the customs declaration say?
- Is the outer packaging plain, and is there any branding on it?
- Who handles fulfilment — you, or a third party?

A retailer that answers these quickly and specifically is usually one that has
done the work. A retailer that answers with a marketing sentence — "100% discreet
shipping!" — has not told you anything at all.
